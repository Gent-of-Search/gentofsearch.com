You Can't Grow Large Websites by Just Publishing Content at Scale.

Publishing more pages used to be enough. Ship them, get them indexed, watch traffic climb. On a 500 page website, that still works.

On a website with 500,000 pages, it doesn't.

Most enterprise SEO teams are now stuck in the same loop. The team is experienced. The audits have been done, often more than once. But organic traffic is flat and nobody can say with confidence why. Core updates get blamed. AI search gets blamed, sometimes fairly. A vague “technical issue” gets blamed. So the team ships more content and more tickets, and the numbers stay where they are.

The problem is rarely knowledge. Enterprise SEO teams usually know what good looks like.

The problem is that at scale you cannot see what is actually broken. Search Console caps exports at 1,000 rows, so a 400,000 page website gets diagnosed through a keyhole. Template level issues hide inside sampled crawls. Pages drop out of Google's index quietly, and by the time the traffic loss reaches a monthly report the cause is months and six deployments old.

And when you do find it, the finding lands in a Google Doc. Engineering is committed to the product roadmap. The SEO ticket has no revenue number attached to it, so it loses every prioritisation call it enters. The work is right. It just never ships.

That's the gap I close.

I work with enterprise teams running large scale websites: ecommerce catalogues, marketplaces, publishers, job and property listings, and platforms carrying a decade of legacy URLs, faceted navigation and programmatic pages.

I don't hand over a document and leave. I diagnose what is actually constraining growth across your full URL set, quantify it so it survives a prioritisation meeting, and work alongside your engineering, product and content teams until it is live.

If your team is experienced and your organic traffic is still flat, another audit won't fix it. What fixes it is a senior pair of hands who can find the real constraint at scale, prove it matters, and stay on it until your team has shipped the solution.

That's where I come in.

I've Helped SEO Teams Across Different Types of Enterprise Websites.

Marketplace

I've worked on marketplaces with millions of listing URLs, most of which Google had never indexed. With supply changing daily, I focused crawl budget on fresh inventory instead of expired listings — getting new pages into search in days, not weeks.

Travel

I've untangled travel sites where destination, property and date combinations spun up near-identical pages at enormous scale. I've reined in the faceted navigation that was multiplying URL counts, so the pages that actually convert stopped getting buried.

Ecommerce

I've managed large catalogues where variants, filters and pagination created far more URLs than there were products. I've cleared discontinued and out-of-stock lines out of the index and rebuilt category pages that had drifted below Google's quality threshold.

Publishing

I've worked with publishers whose decades of archive were competing with today's article for crawl attention. I've helped decide what to prune, refresh or keep, so older content stopped dragging evergreen traffic down with it.

Real Estate

I've handled property portals where listings churned in weeks under location templates running into the tens of thousands. I've made the calls on which pages stay indexed once a property comes off the market — and protected the location pages built on top of them.

Job Listings

I've worked on job boards with the fastest churn of any large website. A role that takes three weeks to index may as well never have existed, so I focused on getting Google to crawl and index new listings fast enough to matter.

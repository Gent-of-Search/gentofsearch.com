You Can't Grow Your Revenue by Just Adding More Product Pages.

Expanding your product catalogue and publishing content used to be enough. Add the products, build out the categories, publish the buying guides, watch traffic climb. For years that was the playbook, and it worked.
Not anymore.

Most ecommerce teams are now stuck in the same loop. Every week new SKUs are published but organic traffic is flat and organic revenue is declining. Core updates get blamed. AI Overviews get blamed, sometimes fairly. A vague “technical issue” gets blamed. So the team does the only thing it knows how to do. It adds more SKUs and content. And nothing changes.

The problem is rarely the size of the catalogue.

It's that nobody has connected the strategy, the technical foundations and the implementation into a single plan that actually ships. The audit sits in a Google Doc. Dev is buried in the replatform, so SEO tickets never make the sprint. Merchandising changes the navigation without anyone checking what it does to crawling. New products land on category pages Google barely visits. The work happens. The revenue doesn't.

That's the gap I close.

I work with ecommerce brands whose SEO channel has stalled — from fast-growing websites to enterprise retailers carrying millions of URLs, faceted navigation, discontinued products and a decade of legacy category structures.

I don't hand over a document and leave. I find what's actually holding growth back, build a prioritised plan around it, and work alongside your dev, merchandising and content teams until it's shipped.

If organic revenue isn't growing, the constraint isn't your output. It's whatever nobody has diagnosed yet — and staying on it until your team has fixed it is the job.
